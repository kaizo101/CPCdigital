## Causal follow-up: the two large shifts

On 3 October, both cells were rerun for 3,000 hands with identical seeds and
one temporary code ablation at a time. The ablation switches were removed
afterward; no production scoring or target was changed. These are
whole-session counterfactuals, so altered early decisions also change later
game states. They identify influential mechanisms, not an exact per-hand
attribution.

| Cell and isolated ablation | Measured rate | Other relevant effect |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| NLHE LAG full ring, current | Fold-to-C-bet 764/1306 = 58.50% | Turn C-bet 122/256 = 47.66% |
| Ignore new opponent-range adjustments | 731/1318 = 55.46% | Turn C-bet 126/252 = 50.00% |
| Restore former full C-bet raise candidate scale | 630/1216 = 51.81% | Turn C-bet 71/156 = 45.51% |
| Both of the preceding ablations | 598/1233 = 48.50% | Turn C-bet 75/153 = 49.02% |
| Ignore new paired-board two-pair classification | 762/1318 = 57.81% | Turn C-bet 117/272 = 43.01% |
| PLO TAG heads-up, current | 3-bet 455/1604 = 28.37% | PFR 34.32% |
| Ignore new opponent-range adjustments | 391/1604 = 24.38% | PFR 33.25% |
| Restore former full reraise-depth penalty | 419/1604 = 26.12% | PFR 33.72% |
| Both of the preceding ablations | 307/1604 = 19.14% | PFR 31.85% |

For NLHE LAG, the foundation changed the C-bet-defense raise candidate from
the full eligible scale to 0.55 for made weak hands, 0.65 for unmade weak
hands, 0.70 for marginal, and 0.60 for medium. That materially suppresses a
formerly common third option (raising), leaving more folds. The opponent
range adjustment adds a smaller effect. The depth mismatch between the
metric and the C-bet-defense recognizer is **not** the main cause: in the
current 3k run, 1293 of 1306 fold-to-C-bet opportunities occurred at flop
aggression depth one; just 13 were later decisions. Paired-board
classification likewise explains little of this particular fold drift.
Restoring the former full raise scale is **not** a ready fix: it sharply
reduces turn-C-bet opportunities (256 to 156) and worsens the already-low
turn-C-bet rate. The same-seed combined result is still just outside the
40–48% fold corridor and cannot by itself justify a rollback.

For PLO TAG heads-up, the two interacting changes almost exactly recreate
the v0.8.1 3-bet rate (19.14% counterfactual versus 19.20% historical).
The new position-aware opponent range moves a button open-raiser from line
score 60 (`strong`) below the discrete `strong` cutoff of 60: a late-position
adjustment of -5 is blended by skill, but even its tiniest nonzero value
crosses the boundary to `moderate`. That drops the raise's opponent-range
penalty from -5 to 0. The new continuous aggression-depth gate also reduces
the old full reraise penalty for bots below skill 100. Both effects favor
reraising, especially medium and good hands that were near the action-choice
boundary. This is an actual continuity problem in the range-read pathway:
a gradual skill weight is followed by an abrupt bucket-to-score jump. It
does **not** mean the old full reraise penalty should simply be restored;
that would bypass the intended skill-dependent perception model.

**Next decision:** review a small sample of the changed medium/good PLO
3-bets and weak/marginal NLHE C-bet defenses for poker plausibility, then
design a narrow continuity fix for range-read score modifiers and a
selective NLHE LAG defense mix. Test each against all formats and adjacent
metrics before considering baseline updates. Keep corridor deviations
non-blocking unless they indicate a material gameplay defect.

### Hand-level plausibility check (3 October 2026)

The same deterministic 3k seeds were inspected at the decision level with
private cards, public board/price, objective and perceived hand assessment,
and candidate utilities. The temporary per-hand sampler was removed after
inspection. A few selected hands are **examples**, not a frequency estimate;
the category/position counts below are complete for the 3k run.

**PLO TAG heads-up:** The 455/1604 3-bet rate mixes two distinct situations.
From the big blind against a button open, TAG 3-bets **257/1241 = 20.71%**.
From the button after first limping and then facing a big-blind raise, TAG
limp-reraises **198/363 = 54.55%**. The latter contributes 43.5% of all
3-bets despite only 22.6% of opportunities. It is the primary gameplay
plausibility concern, not a claim that every heads-up 3-bet is excessive.

Selected button limp-reraises illustrate the composition:

| Hand | TAG cards | Objective class and candidate utilities | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #35 | Q♥ J♠ 5♥ 5♦ | medium; call 52.51, raise 53.16 | Single-suited with a low pair and little four-card coordination. A frequent TAG reraise is hard to justify, although one mixed aggressive decision is possible. |
| #45 | A♥ J♦ 9♠ 2♠ | medium; call 52.76, raise 63.16 | Ace is not in the playable suit; the spade flush is only 9-high and the 2 is a dangler. This is the clearest weak-structure reraise in the sample. |
| #53 | 9♦ 5♦ 4♥ 3♠ | medium; call 51.90, raise 53.16 | Low three-card connection plus a 9-high suit, with weak nut potential. More natural as a cheap call or fold than a routine TAG reraise. |
| #58 | A♣ Q♣ Q♥ 5♠ | good; call 51.01, raise 56.14 | High pair and ace-high usable suit make a value reraise plausible. The diagnosis must preserve hands like this. |
| #68 | J♦ 9♦ 8♦ 7♥ | good; call 51.63, raise 55.14 | Connected ranks, but three cards share one suit; this is a mixed/fragile candidate, not equivalent to a double-suited rundown. |

In #35/#53, `Late-position leverage` adds +15 to the raise even though the
button has already chosen a limp; `PLO heads-up — widen preflop initiative`
adds another +5. The TAG preflop table prefers a **call** with medium hands
(+10 to call, 0 to raise), but that preference is overcome. Meanwhile the
blind raiser's baseline line score of 60 (`strong`) becomes about 58.74
(`moderate`) after the skill-weighted blind-position adjustment in these
hands, removing the -5 raise modifier. The aggression-depth penalty is only
about -5.45 rather than the former -8. These mechanisms explain why weakly
structured hands enter a near-tie or raise-favoured selection window. The
general PLO hand-selection dimensions in the existing
[preflop abstraction audit](../docs/en/reviews/plo-preflop-abstraction-audit-2026-10-01.md)
and [Upswing's PLO discussion](https://upswingpoker.com/podcast/ep19-pot-limit-omaha/)
support checking nut potential, connectivity, suitedness and pair quality
separately; neither source supplies a validated 3-bet target for these exact
heads-up spots. The poker judgement above is therefore a flagged design
assessment, **not** a solver verdict or a proposed hand chart.

**NLHE LAG full ring:** Of 1306 C-bet defence opportunities, 764 ended in a
fold: **468/520 air**, **296/606 weak**, and **0/180 marginal-or-better**.
Among the weak folds, 139 were unmade/no-draw and 139 were made/no-draw;
15 unmade/draw and three made/draw folds completed the total. Fold frequency
rose with the call-to-pot price: 13/62 below 25%, 307/618 at 25–40%,
434/615 at 40–60%, and 10/11 at 60% or more. Note that the generic `made`
flag can include a pair shared by the board; it is not proof that a private
card improved the hand.

Concrete folds include K♠ J♥ on 6♠ 7♥ T♦ facing 380 with pot 430 (#8),
and A♥ 6♠ on 2♦ 5♥ 7♣ facing 130 with pot 180 and two opponents (#18):
neither supplies an obvious reason to force a continue. K♥ J♥ on A♥ K♠ 7♦
facing 240 with pot 390 (#43) is a genuine borderline middle-pair spot, but
fold 49.75 and call 47.24 both lie in the selection window; this was not a
hard-coded compulsory fold. The sample therefore does not support blindly
recreating the former high-frequency weak-hand C-bet raises or adding a
global call bonus. The corridor drift remains worth triaging, but the sampled
folds are mostly understandable and no clear NLHE rule bug was found.

**Cut decision after the separate PLO correction:** retain the NLHE LAG
full-ring behaviour for 0.8.2 as a documented gameplay watch item. The
10k primary and independent rates (57.60% and 56.92%) are a real departure
from the 40–48% guide, not dismissed as 300-hand noise. However, in the 3k
decision breakdown 468/520 Air and 296/606 Weak defence opportunities folded,
while none of 180 Marginal-or-better opportunities did. Only 18 of the 296
weak folds carried a draw; the reviewed individual folds faced meaningful
prices. Restoring the former full C-bet raise-candidate scale reduced folds
but also reduced turn-C-bet opportunities from 256 to 156 and worsened the
already-low turn-C-bet rate. This does not establish a gameplay gain or a
specific rule defect. The target corridor and foundation snapshot remain
unchanged, and no compensatory score edit is made. Reopen on reproducible
folds of clear equity/value hands or a stronger hand-level pattern, not on
the aggregate guide miss alone.

**Narrow next step:** Address the PLO button limp-reraise pathway first:
preserve legitimate high-pair/nut-suit value hands, but check whether generic
late-position leverage should still apply after a limp and remove the hard
range-strength bucket cliff. Use focused legal-state tests for the contrasting
hands above, then rerun all PLO formats and adjacent metrics. Treat NLHE LAG
as a separate gameplay/corridor decision rather than forcing its percentage
down in the same patch.

### PLO TAG heads-up button limp-reraise correction (3 October)

The narrow follow-up now identifies the spot using PLO, TAG, fixed heads-up
table size, button/late position, one prior raise and the bot's own earlier
preflop call. After this limp, the generic late-position raise bonus is scaled
from +15 to +3 for a hand below `good` with neither four-card coordination nor
an ace-high usable suit. A perceived coordinated rundown or nut suit gradually
restores the full bonus; `good` or stronger hands retain it. The existing +5
PLO heads-up initiative bonus and skill-dependent aggression-depth rule are
unchanged. This is a local score correction, not an opening chart or a hard
ban on bluff reraises.

For this one PLO TAG heads-up pathway, the opponent's numerical range score
now interpolates action modifiers at 30/45/60/75 instead of jumping when the
human-readable label changes. Thus a blind-raiser score of 58.74 applies about
-4.58 to the raise rather than abruptly changing from -5 to zero. Unknown
or folded ranges remain neutral. Other archetypes, formats and NLHE retain
their existing modifiers pending a separate review.

### PLO deep-call and eligible-pot price checks (3 October)

The NLHE first-encounter 100 BB open-shove example is not directly legal in
PLO. With 10/20 blinds, six 2,000-chip stacks and no earlier action, the
PLO engine caps the first raise at 70 chips (3.5 BB); an immediate 2,000-chip
all-in is unavailable and rejected. In a heads-up 100 BB first hand, four
successive pot-sized raises reach 60, 180, 540 and 1,620. The next player
faces a 1,080 call only after previously putting in 540, including 530
voluntary chips (26.5% of the 2,000 starting stack). The actual price is
1,080/(2,160 + 1,080) = 33.33%. This is a committed escalation decision,
not the NLHE case of an uncommitted player facing an instant 100 BB open
shove. A matching bot-decision regression with that legal price records 26.5%
voluntary commitment and 1,080/1,460 = 73.97% forced-all-in ratio. A weakly
structured medium PLO hand rates folding above calling, while a premium
double-suited ace pair rates calling above folding. Neither receives the
NLHE-only deep-call pressure factor. Existing PLO escalation scoring and
legal-action guards are retained.

For a caller with only 40 chips facing 100, the engine caps the payable call
at 40. From a 200-chip total pot, only 140 is eligible, so the true price is
40/(140 + 40) = 22.22%, not 40/(200 + 40) = 16.67%. The same engine test now
runs under both NLHE and PLO. A client metric regression also preserves the
historical PLO short-stack example: 0.80 payable from an eligible 2.62 pot
gives 0.80/3.42 = 23.39%, rather than using the unreachable full 3.02 pot.
It records the forced-all-in ratio (100%) independently from voluntary
investment. A separate PLO scorer counter-case confirms that a call priced
below 10% receives no forced-all-in variance penalty merely because it uses
the entire remaining stack. No mathematical price error or variant-specific
score bug was found in these cases, so there is no further scoring change.

## 3 October: deep preflop calls and first-encounter overbets

Manual debug session `S20261003T114244510Z`, hand 1, revealed a separate
release-relevant decision gap. Facing the hero's raise to 2,000 at 10/20,
Alva held Q♦ J♥ in the big blind and had to call 1,980 into a 2,090 pot.
The required share of the final 4,070 pot was **48.65%**. The bot scored
fold 0, call 59.52, and passive all-in call 58.32; the chosen all-in was a
call, not an independent aggressive shove. `strong` represented QJo's
starting-hand percentile, not an equity estimate against a deep shove range.
The existing forced-all-in risk factor was zero for `strong` hands. This
was an actionable scoring gap, not a pot-award or legality error. The
`bluff-catch` tag on Nika's *unchosen* Q♠ 7♣ call candidate was a generic
preflop-medium intent label, not evidence that Nika detected a bluff.

Regression tests reproduced that call and a second first-encounter gap: an
NLHE TAG with K♠ J♥ on K♦ 7♣ 2♥ still called a three-pot flop bet with no
prior sizing read. The fix adds a bounded, continuous price-risk factor for
uncommitted deep NLHE preflop calls and for non-nut NLHE one-/two-pair hands
facing more than a pot-sized effective call price. It does **not** estimate
hidden cards or force all large bets to be value. Skill and archetype still
moderate the factor; prior commitment progressively reduces the preflop
effect. Normal-sized bets, AA/KK value continues, a flop set, and strong
draws are guarded by contrasting tests. The generic medium preflop-call
intent is now `pot-control`, not `bluff-catch`.

The same postflop rule was **not** transferred to PLO. In a direct 1,000-hand
same-seed PLO Calling Station 6-max comparison, adding that generic factor
raised fold-to-C-bet from **266/655 = 40.61%** to **296/655 = 45.19%** and
reduced turn C-bet from **25/122 = 20.49%** to **20/114 = 17.54%**. This
materially worsens two pre-existing diagnostic deviations. The temporary
ablation switch was removed; a PLO-specific overbet defence would require
hand-level evidence and separate design. In PLO, an expensive *eligible-pot*
call can occur even under pot-limit rules when the caller is short and cannot
contest an entire side pot; the factor cannot simply be dismissed as dead
code. First-hand PLO shove calls and eligible-pot overbet decisions remain
separate hand-level review items, not validated by this NLHE fix. A same-seed
NLHE LAG full-ring
1,000-hand check changed fold-to-C-bet only from **248/426 = 58.22%** to
**250/427 = 58.55%**; its prior large drift remains a separate review.
Neither target corridor nor the 300-hand foundation snapshot was moved.

A fresh 300-hand-per-cell smoke covered all 24 NLHE/PLO archetype/format
combinations after the narrowed fix. Every cell exited normally with zero
invalid-action fallbacks and no structural violations; diagnostic corridor
misses remain, including the pre-existing PLO TAG heads-up 3-bet rate
**46/156 = 29.49%**. PLO Calling Station 6-max returned the pre-fix
**78/201 = 38.81%** fold-to-C-bet and **5/35 = 14.29%** turn C-bet.
At matched seeds and stack depths, 100-hand NLHE/PLO TAG 6-max metrics and

## 3 October: foundation snapshot refresh decision

The release preflight's 300-hand regression still compared the current code
with the snapshot taken before the documented PLO TAG heads-up limp-reraise
correction and NLHE deep-call defence. It reported six percentage-point drift
errors. Four are the already triaged small-denominator alerts in the opening
table (NLHE TAG heads-up turn C-bet, PLO Nit heads-up fold-to-C-bet, PLO LAG
six-max turn C-bet, and PLO Calling Station six-max turn C-bet). Their
counter-samples and the Calling Station watch item remain as recorded there.

The other two are PLO TAG heads-up 3-bet (29.49% to 16.67% in the 300-hand
